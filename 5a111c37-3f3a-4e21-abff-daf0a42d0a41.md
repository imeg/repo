## CHA2DS2-VASc score
We use the CHA2DS2-VASc score (calculator 1)  (table 1) to estimate thromboembolic risk in patients with AF, while recognizing its limitations (see'Potential alternatives'below and'Limitations'below). This estimation of baseline thromboembolic risk is combined with the above information on relative risk reduction (see'General efficacy'above) to estimate the expected absolute risk reduction.
### Stroke risk based on scores

- The annual risk of ischemic stroke in untreated patients is estimated to be 0.2, 0.6, and 2.2 percent for those with CHA2DS2-VASc scores of 0, 1, and 2, respectively  (table 2) [13]. However, stroke rates have varied substantially among studies, which may be due to differences in study populations and methodologies [14-19]. As an example, studies examining ischemic stroke rates in patients with a single risk factor have identified risks of <1 to 2.7 percent/year [20-22].

- Among patients with AF, ischemic stroke is the dominant type of thromboembolic event. As an example, in a study including data on 39,973 participants in four randomized trials of anticoagulation, the incidence of nonstroke systemic embolic events (SEEs) was 0.23/100 person-years, and the incidence of cerebral embolism was 1.92/100 person-years [23]. Among those with nonstroke SEEs, 58 percent occurred in the lower extremities, 31 percent in the visceral-mesenteric system, and 11 percent in the upper extremities.

- Among patients with AF treated with OAC, annual stroke risk is lowered by approximately two-thirds to <0.1, 0.2, and 0.6 percent for CHA2DS2-VASc scores of 0, 1, and 2, respectively. In addition to the lowering of stroke risk, there is evidence thatwarfarin, compared with no anticoagulant therapy, leads to less severe stroke episodes and lower 30-day stroke mortality [8,24].

- The annual risk of intracranial bleeding with VKA (warfarin) is 0.2, 0.3, and 0.5 percent for CHA2DS2-VASc scores of 0, 1, and 2, respectively. The risk of ICH with DOAC is approximately half of that with VKA  (table 3).

### Recommendations based on score
Among adults with AF, females have a modestly higher risk of thromboembolism than males, but female sex is associated with increased risk primarily in patients with at least two CHA2DS2-VASc score non-sex risk score points [25,26]. Thus, we focus on non-sex risk factors when deciding whether OAC is indicated.
- **For CHA****2****DS****2****-VASc score ≥2 in males or ≥3 in females**(two or more risk score points from non-sex risk factors), thromboembolic risk is high and we recommend long-term OAC. The benefit from OAC generally exceeds the risks of severe bleeding [13,27-29].

- **For CHA****2D****S****2-****V****AS****c score of 1 in males or 2 in females**(one non-sex risk factor with a value of 1), thromboembolic risk is intermediate. We consider the heterogeneity of thromboembolic risk, and the burden of AF, in employing shared decision-making with patients in deciding whether to initiate anticoagulation.

- The magnitude of thromboembolism risk imparted by CHA2DS2-VASc risk factors is heterogenous [26,30]. Among the non-sex risk factors with a one-point value, age 65 to 74 years confers greater thromboembolic risk, and some contributors would initiate anticoagulation on that basis [26,31].

- We additionally consider the burden of AF in these patients with intermediate thromboembolic risk. For patients with very low burden of AF (eg, AF that is well documented as limited to an isolated episode that may have been due to a reversible cause such as recent surgery, heavy alcohol ingestion, or sleep deprivation), it may be reasonable to forgo long-term OAC and institute close surveillance for recurrent AF, although it may not be possible to reliably estimate AF burden from surveying symptoms or infrequent monitoring. ### Potential alternatives
A variety of risk scores, imaging methods, and biomarkers have been studied for their potential predictive value for thromboembolic risk in patients with AF [30,36]. The clinical utility of a risk score for individuals with AF hinges primarily on its accuracy in identifying those at lowest risk for thromboembolism, as OAC is generally recommended for individuals with all but the lowest level of risk.
The CHA2DS2-VASc score is considered the most validated risk score [30,36]. The CHA2DS2-VASc score provides improved risk discrimination compared with the original CHADS2score  (table 2andtable 1). The ATRIA risk score  (table 4) and the GARFIELD-AF score are potential alternatives to the CHA2DS2-VASc score, which modestly improve risk discrimination but have not been as rigorously evaluated as the CHA2DS2-VASc score [30]. All these risk scores are subject to the limitations discussed below. (See'Limitations'below.)
A potential alternative to the risk score approach is to calculate the risk for each patient based upon risk factors including age as a continuous variable using theCalculator of Absolute Stroke Risk (CARS)[26].
For patients with AF, there is no established role for routine cardiac imaging to assess thromboembolic risk.
